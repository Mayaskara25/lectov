1. THE BASIC CONCEPT: WHAT IS TRIGONOMETRY?
-------------------------------------------
Trigonometry is the study of relationships between the sides and angles
of a triangle. The word comes from Greek: 'trigon' (triangle) and
'metron' (measure).

In Class 10, we restrict our study strictly to RIGHT-ANGLED TRIANGLES.
Imagine a triangle ABC, where Angle B = 90 degrees. We choose one of
the acute angles, say Angle A, and call it 'theta' (written as a zero
with a horizontal line).

How we define the three sides depends entirely on where 'theta' is:
* HYPOTENUSE (H): The longest side. It is always opposite the 90° angle.
* PERPENDICULAR (P): The side directly opposite to our chosen angle theta.
* BASE (B): The side next to (adjacent to) angle theta, which forms the
  floor of the angle along with the hypotenuse.

CRITICAL TIP: If you switch your attention to Angle C, the Perpendicular
and Base swap places! The side opposite to the angle is ALWAYS the
Perpendicular.


2. THE SIX TRIGONOMETRIC RATIOS (WHY DO THEY EXIST?)
----------------------------------------------------
A ratio is just one side divided by another side. Since a triangle has
3 sides, there are exactly 6 different ways to divide them. We give
each fraction a specific name:

* sin(theta)   = Perpendicular / Hypotenuse       [P/H]
* cos(theta)   = Base / Hypotenuse                [B/H]
* tan(theta)   = Perpendicular / Base             [P/B]
* cosec(theta) = Hypotenuse / Perpendicular     [H/P]
* sec(theta)   = Hypotenuse / Base                [H/B]
* cot(theta)   = Base / Perpendicular             [B/P]

MEMORY TRICK FOR THE ORDER:
"Pandit Badri Prasad Har Har Bole"
  P/H (sin)  |  B/H (cos)  |  P/B (tan)
  ------------------------------------
  H/P (cosec)|  H/B (sec)  |  B/P (cot)

REAL-WORLD MEANING:
If sin(30°) = 1/2, it means that for any right triangle with a 30-degree
angle, the opposite wall (Perpendicular) will always be exactly half the
length of the sloped ramp (Hypotenuse), no matter how big or small the
triangle is!


3. RECIPROCAL RELATIONSHIPS (HOW THEY CONNECT)
----------------------------------------------
Look closely at the formulas above. You will notice that some are just
flipped versions of others. These are called reciprocal identities:

* cosec(theta) is the exact upside-down version of sin(theta).
  Formula: cosec(theta) = 1 / sin(theta)

* sec(theta) is the exact upside-down version of cos(theta).
  Formula: sec(theta) = 1 / cos(theta)

* cot(theta) is the exact upside-down version of tan(theta).
  Formula: cot(theta) = 1 / tan(theta)

* Division connection: If you divide sin by cos, the 'H' cancels out
  and leaves you with P/B, which is tan!
  Formula: tan(theta) = sin(theta) / cos(theta)
  Formula: cot(theta) = cos(theta) / sin(theta)


4. THE THREE CHIEF TRIGONOMETRIC IDENTITIES
-------------------------------------------
An identity is an equation that holds true for any value of theta you
plug into it. They are derived directly using Pythagoras Theorem
(P^2 + B^2 = H^2).

Identity 1: sin^2(theta) + cos^2(theta) = 1
* Meaning: Square the sin value, square the cos value, add them, and
  the answer is ALWAYS 1.
* Useful Variations: sin^2 = 1 - cos^2   OR   cos^2 = 1 - sin^2

Identity 2: 1 + tan^2(theta) = sec^2(theta)
* Useful Variations: sec^2 - tan^2 = 1   OR   tan^2 = sec^2 - 1

Identity 3: 1 + cot^2(theta) = cosec^2(theta)
* Useful Variations: cosec^2 - cot^2 = 1   OR   cot^2 = cosec^2 - 1


5. THE SPECIFIC ANGLES TABLE (EXPLANATION)
------------------------------------------
In your board exams, you will solve problems using standard geometric
angles (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°). You must memorize these values.

Angle (deg) | 0°    | 30°      | 45°      | 60°      | 90°
------------|-------|----------|----------|----------|-----
sin(theta)  | 0     | 1/2      | 1/sqrt(2)| sqrt(3)/2| 1
cos(theta)  | 1     | sqrt(3)/2| 1/sqrt(2)| 1/2      | 0
tan(theta)  | 0     | 1/sqrt(3)| 1        | sqrt(3)  | Not Defined
cosec(theta)| N.D.  | 2        | sqrt(2)  | 2/sqrt(3)| 1
sec(theta)  | 1     | 2/sqrt(3)| sqrt(2)  | 2        | Not Defined
cot(theta)  | N.D.  | sqrt(3)  | 1        | 1/sqrt(3)| 0

UNDERSTANDING "NOT DEFINED" (N.D.):
At 90°, the Base of the triangle shrinks down to 0. Since you cannot
divide any number by zero in mathematics, ratios like tan(90°) [P/B]
and sec(90°) [H/B] become mathematically impossible to calculate. We
call this "Not Defined".

HOW TO RECONSTRUCT THIS TABLE IN 1 MINUTE IN THE EXAM HALL:
1. Write down numbers 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 for the columns 0° to 90°.
2. Divide all of them by 4: (0/4, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4) -> (0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1).
3. Take the square root of each: (0, 1/2, 1/sqrt(2), sqrt(3)/2, 1).
4. THIS IS YOUR SIN ROW!
5. Reverse the sin row from right to left to get your COS ROW.
6. Divide Sin by Cos to get your TAN ROW.
